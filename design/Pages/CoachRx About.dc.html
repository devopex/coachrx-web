CoachRx was built from inside the coaching relationship, by a company that has spent over 30 years coaching individuals and educating coaches to deliver Individual Design Coaching, a method we defined. The origin

Why a coaching company built its own software.

For over 30 years the work has looked the same. Assess the person in front of you. Understand the life they are living. Design for the years ahead, not just the week ahead. Coach the whole person, not just their training. OPEX has been doing that with clients and educating it to coaches since 1999.

That work has never fit the industry's tools. Coaches doing it end up stitching it together themselves: a programming app here, a spreadsheet for nutrition, a notes app for the conversations that actually move the client. The tools were built to deliver workouts. Workouts are what the wider fitness culture asks for. The coaching kept happening in the gaps.

So we built CoachRx. Assessment, program design, nutrition, lifestyle, communication, and the business behind it, in one place. Every feature is available from the first day of your trial, because a coach should not have to upgrade to do the job properly.

CoachRx does not prescribe a philosophy. Plenty of coaches on the platform have never taken an OPEX course, and it never asks them to. What it assumes is that you are coaching individuals, one at a time, and that you would rather spend your hours on the person than on the admin.

The platform is now led by the engineer who built the first coaching app the industry adopted at scale. He started as an OPEX coaching client and returned to OPEX and CoachRx to build the version he could not build then, on top of coaching principles that were already decades proven.

What we stand for

What we stand for

Coaching is a profession, not a side hustle or a content channel. These are the beliefs behind what we build. They are not rules for how you coach.

Person before program Understand who you are coaching before you decide what they should do. The assessment is the reason the program works.
Decades over quarters Real change is slow. Planning a year should be as easy as planning a week.
Individual over generic No two clients are the same. Their fitness and lifestyle should not be either.
Standards over shortcuts Doing it properly takes longer. It is also the only version that holds.
Relationship over interface Every feature gets judged on one question. Does this put the coach closer to the client, or further away?
Independence over dependence The best coaching leaves a client able to carry it themselves. We build for coaches who want that.
