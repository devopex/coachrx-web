Effective coaches understand that resistance training progression extends far beyond simply adding weight to the bar. When coaching clients through their resistance training journey, a structured approach to progression based on training age, individual capacity, and specific goals creates sustainable results.

In an AMA video (see below), James FitzGerald of OPEX Fitness shared valuable insights on resistance training progression that align with what successful coaches implement in their practice. Let's explore these concepts and how to apply them in your coaching.

### **Beyond Traditional Progression Methods**

Experienced coaches recognize multiple pathways for progression beyond just increasing reps and volume:

- **Rest Period Manipulation**: Strategically shortening recovery between sets challenges the client differently than simply adding weight
- **Tempo Variation**: Altering the speed of different movement phases (eccentric, concentric, isometric) creates new training stimuli
- **Exercise Selection Evolution**: Progressing movement patterns through exercise variations maintains novelty while building skill
- **Advanced Technique Implementation**: Adding methods like drop sets or strip sets when appropriate for advanced clients

These varied approaches allow coaches to create individualized progression paths based on client needs and capabilities.

#### **Individualizing Progression Based on Training Age**

Professional coaches adjust progression strategies according to each client's training experience:

**Beginner Clients**

For novice trainees, effective coaches prioritize:

- Motor control development before load increases
- Movement pattern consistency and quality
- Technical proficiency in fundamental exercises
- Building capacity through appropriate volume

**Intermediate Clients**

As clients advance, skilled coaches implement:

- Progressive overload while maintaining technical quality
- Balanced focus on strength endurance and motor control
- Strategic intensity increases while monitoring recovery
- More varied exercise selection to challenge movement patterns

**Advanced Clients**

With experienced clients, coaches typically:

- Focus on maximum contractions in appropriate phases
- Carefully program strength endurance work
- Continue refining motor control and movement efficiency
- Implement more sophisticated progression models

**Long-Term Development**

The most successful coaches always keep the 30-50 year perspective in mind, prioritizing movement longevity over short-term gains.

#### **RPE Implementation: A Coaching Tool**

Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE) serves as a valuable biofeedback tool when used appropriately. Effective coaches understand:

- RPE works best with clients who have developed body awareness
- Beginners often need more structured approaches before using RPE effectively
- Proper RPE usage requires client education and consistent check-ins
- RPE should complement, not replace, objective measures of progress

#### **Best Practices for Sustainable Progress**

The most effective resistance training progression follows these principles:

- Consistency and quality movement take precedence over rapid load increases
- Progression should match what the client can genuinely express and recover from
- Different progression variables (load, volume, tempo, rest) should be manipulated one at a time
- Long-term movement quality must never be sacrificed for short-term progression

As James FitzGerald emphasizes, the ultimate goal is lifelong movement capacity—not just impressive numbers for a season or two.
